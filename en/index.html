The Science

How β-TCP becomes bone

β-tricalcium phosphate is one of the phosphate ceramics that most closely simulates the mineral structure of bone. It dissolves gradually in the body and is replaced by new bone as it resorbs.

Osteoconductive scaffold

Interconnected micro- and macropores form a temporary scaffold that cells and blood vessels can grow into.

Calcium and phosphate ions

As the graft dissolves it releases calcium and phosphate ions that support new bone formation. Every form contains 1% silicon dioxide (silicate).

Complete bioresorption

Over time the porous structure is fully infiltrated and replaced by healthy, viable bone. No permanent foreign material is left at the healed site.

Frequently Asked Questions

Veterinary bone grafts: common questions

Answers are based on the product instructions for use (IFU). For case-specific questions, message us on WhatsApp.

What is a synthetic bone graft?

A synthetic bone graft is a biomaterial, free of human or animal tissue, used to fill bone defects and act as a scaffold for new bone formation. RebonaVet is a β-tricalcium phosphate (β-TCP) based synthetic bone graft with 1% silicate, made for veterinary use only.

Why is β-TCP used in veterinary orthopaedics?

β-TCP closely mimics the mineral structure of bone; it is biocompatible and osteoconductive. It dissolves gradually in the body and is replaced by the patient’s own bone. Being synthetic, it needs no donor site and carries no risk of disease transmission.

Which patients is RebonaVet used in?

It is used by veterinary surgeons in small animal orthopaedics and traumatology, mainly dogs and cats, to fill non-load-bearing bone defects in fracture repair, non-union, osteotomy, arthrodesis, revision surgery, bone cysts and tumour voids, and spinal fusion.

How long does the graft take to resorb?

According to the instructions for use, Granule and Putty keep their structure for 16–24 weeks and are fully resorbed within 6–12 months. With the Flexible form, activity is largely complete in 4–6 months; full resorption may take up to 18 months.

Which form should I choose?

Putty is applied straight from the syringe, which makes it practical for narrow and irregular voids. Granule is used for cavity filling and for mixing with bone marrow aspirate. Stick and Block give measured, controlled placement. Flexible is for sites where the graft has to conform to anatomy.

Can it be mixed with bone marrow aspirate or autograft?

Yes. The Granule and Flexible forms can be used together with bone marrow aspirate and other clinically known bone grafts.

Can it be used in load-bearing sites?

No. RebonaVet is intended only for non-load-bearing bone defects; it does not provide structural support to the skeleton and must not be used to gain screw fixation. Fracture stability should be provided by an implant system such as plates and screws.

Are the products sterile, and how are they stored?

All products are sterilised by validated gamma irradiation (SAL 10⁻⁶) and supplied single use in double sterile barrier packaging. Store dry, out of direct sunlight, at +17 °C to +27 °C and 40–60% relative humidity. Shelf life is 5 years for Granule and 3 years for Putty and Flexible.

Is the graft visible on radiographs?

Yes. RebonaVet is radiopaque; Granule and Putty contain 0.1% zirconium oxide (ZrO₂) for radiographic follow-up.
